**2026 is the year indies stopped being the alternative: they outsell the big releases.** Slay the Spire 2 moved three million copies in its first week and Mewgenics recouped its budget in three hours. And there are four complete games you can play today without paying a cent.

> Short answer: if you only try two, make them Slay the Spire 2 (early access, cards and co-op) and Between Stops (free, pure Undertale). They cover both ends of the year.

The 2026 indie scene moves at two speeds: small-studio games competing with blockbusters in sales, and one-person projects giving away entire works. This list goes by category, with prices and platforms, so you can pick by what you have spare: time or money.

## The essentials of 2026

**Slay the Spire 2.** The sequel to the legendary roguelike deckbuilder launched into Early Access in March 2026 and sold over three million copies in its first week. New characters, more than 575 cards, and the big addition: four-player co-op. The full 1.0 is expected in 2027, but what is already there gives you tens of hours. If you poured hundreds of hours into the first one, this is your game of the year.

**Mewgenics.** The thirteen-year project from Edmund McMillen, creator of The Binding of Isaac. It is a tactical roguelike full of cats, dark humor and procedural generation that recouped its budget in three hours and passed a million copies in week one. Reviews have it as the best-rated game of the year so far. It demands patience: the first battle does not explain itself.

**MOUSE: P.I. For Hire.** A first-person shooter with a 1930s cartoon look, set in a gangster noir. It arrives on PC, PS5, Xbox Series and Switch 2, and runs eight to ten hours. It is the most cinematic indie of the year: if you want something short and full of style instead of a hundred-hour game, start here.

**Blue Prince.** An exploration and puzzle adventure for people who enjoy taking notes. It rewards patience and attention, and it is the kind of game you recommend without explaining too much.

## Free, for real (no strings)

**Between Stops.** A one-person RPG, free since May 2026, with a 95% positive rating. It mixes turn-based combat with bullet-hell dodging, branching choices and a look that pays homage to Undertale and Deltarune. It lasts an afternoon and asks for no microtransactions.

**Control, I'm Not Coming Back.** Low-poly science fiction with a *hopecore* aesthetic, also free and sitting at 98% positive. It is short, around an hour, and designed as a reflective trip through deep space. The price is zero, so length is not an argument against it.

**Mosaic Retrospective.** A giant free nonogram where each section comes with headlines and viral moments from the previous year. It is the kind of puzzle you can leave half-finished and come back to, with difficulty adjustable on the fly.

**We Were Here.** A two-player co-op escape room: one reads the clues and describes them, the other interprets them, all over walkie-talkie. The base game is free and works with any friend who can talk to you by voice. The communication is the mechanic, not a bonus.

And if you want the classic that still works: **Doki Doki Literature Club**, free, which pretends to be a school visual novel until it stops.

## What's still coming

- **Witchbrook.** The magic life sim published by the Stardew Valley label: a witch academy, friendships and farming with spells. Long wait, huge expectations.
- **Big Walk.** From the creators of Untitled Goose Game: a huge island to explore with friends using proximity chat and co-op puzzles.
- **Coromon: Rogue Planet.** A roguelite spin-off of the monster-tamer Coromon, with 15+ generated biomes and 130 creatures.

## Where to buy them cheap (and safely)

For the paid ones, the usual play is simple: **wishlist on Steam and wait for the sales**. Indies discount often, often below $10, and comparators like gg.deals will alert you to the historical low across official stores.

Note "official": avoid grey-market key shops. A revoked key means losing the game, and five dollars saved is not worth it. Steam, Humble or Fanatical are safe.

As for platforms, almost everything here comes to PC and much of it is verified for Steam Deck. On console, check the store page first: several picks of the year (MOUSE, for example) launch simultaneously, but others arrive months later.

## What to play depending on your mood

- **Addictive, one more run:** Slay the Spire 2, Mewgenics.
- **Little time, lots of style:** MOUSE: P.I. For Hire, Control, The Supper.
- **With a friend:** We Were Here, Big Walk (soon).
- **Puzzles and brains:** Blue Prince, Mosaic Retrospective.
- **A story that stays:** Between Stops, Doki Doki Literature Club.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are the free games actually free?**
All four here are: no battle pass, no in-game currency, no purchase required to finish. They are sustained by their creators' goodwill or, best case, by donations.

**Is Slay the Spire 2's Early Access worth it?**
Yes if you liked the first: the current content is already enormous and updates are constant. If you prefer the finished, polished experience, wait for 1.0 in 2027.

**Do I need a powerful PC?**
No. Most are 2D or simple-styled games that run fine on any laptop. The exceptions usually say so on their store page.

**Can I play them on my phone?**
Almost none here, unless you buy the mobile ports (Balatro and DDLC have them). PC and console indie still rules the big screen in your pocket.

**What if I don't know where to start?**
Start with a free one. Between Stops costs nothing and gives you a closed story over a weekend. If it hooks you, you already know which genre to return to.

## The year rewards ideas, not budgets

The best thing about indie in 2026 is not that the games are cheap: it is that each one tries something different. A deckbuilder that redesigns itself, an RPG made in a bedroom, a hand-drawn shooter. Money only decides whether you try it this week or in the next sale.

With four free games on the table, "it's too expensive" is no longer an argument.
