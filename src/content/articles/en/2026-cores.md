**A "core" is a visual shortcut: a name for a set of colors, clothes and references.** It's useful for finding ideas, not for becoming someone else.

> Short answer: pick the core that fits your life, your climate and your comfort, not the one that shows up most in your feed.

## What a "core" is (and where it comes from)

The word comes from the English suffix *-core*, used to name subgenres. On the internet it started getting applied to aesthetics: cottagecore, normcore, dark academia. The pattern is always the same: a community shares images, clothes and objects, and the name labels that world.

A core isn't a brand or a rule. It's a box of references. And like any box, you can take what works for you and leave the rest.

## The cores still alive in 2026

There's no official ranking, and anyone who tells you there is is selling something. These are some of the ones still showing up:

- **Clean girl.** Minimalism, neutral tones, glowing skin, slicked-back hair. Easy to wear and easy to mix.
- **Cottagecore.** Long dresses, earth tones, flowers, imaginary countryside. More summer than winter.
- **Dark academia.** Browns, dark greens, blazers, books, libraries. Effortlessly elegant.
- **Light academia.** The lighter version of the above: beige, white, fine knits, afternoon light.
- **Coquette.** Bows, lace, pink, delicate details. Works best in small doses.
- **Y2K.** Low-rise jeans, crop tops, small sunglasses, shine. The most literal core: it copies a specific era.
- **Gorpcore.** Technical mountain gear worn in the city: fleeces, sandals with socks, acid colors.
- **Balletcore.** Tights, ballet flats, ribbons, pastel tones. Very visual and not very practical in the rain.
- **Normcore.** Normal clothes on purpose: jeans, sweatshirt, sneakers. The core for people who don't want a core.
- **Streetwear.** Oversized hoodies, caps, sneakers, cult brands. Broad and easy to adapt.

The list changes every season. What doesn't change is the logic: every core has a handful of key pieces and a mood.

## How to know which one fits you

You don't have to pick one forever. Ask yourself four questions:

**1. What's your life like?** If you bike everywhere, don't dress like you're going to a period ball. If you work with the public, think about what you can wear for eight hours.

**2. What's your climate like?** A linen cottagecore doesn't work in January in the north. Gorpcore does.

**3. What feels comfortable?** If you can't stand bows, coquette isn't for you. If you hate turtlenecks, dark academia gets complicated.

**4. Which colors suit you?** It isn't a rigid rule, but if beige washes you out, don't make it your uniform.

The honest answer is usually a mix: 70% of a core that feels natural and 30% of another one that's fun.

## How to try it without spending

**1. Make a reference board.** Save images you actually like, not the ones you're supposed to like.

**2. Look for the key pieces in your closet.** You almost always already own part of that core without realizing it.

**3. Buy one piece.** Try a single item and wear it for a week. If you feel like you're in costume, it wasn't for you.

**4. Go secondhand.** Vinted, local shops and flea markets are better for testing aesthetics than fast-fashion stores.

**5. Swap with friends.** A core is easier to try when you don't have to buy the whole uniform.

## The costume trap

The most common mistake is buying the entire aesthetic: the dress, the shoes, the bag and the accessory. You wear it once, take the photo and never touch it again.

An aesthetic clicks when **you wear it without thinking**. If you need a manual to get dressed, it probably isn't your style, it's a character.

The test is simple: take the most "core" piece you own and wear it to buy groceries. If you feel ridiculous, save it for specific occasions.

## How to mix cores without looking like a collage

- **Pick an anchor.** One strong piece from the main core and the rest in neutral tones.
- **Repeat one color.** If you mix two aesthetics, make them share at least one color.
- **Watch the proportions.** A coquette top with normcore pants works; a full look from two different cores doesn't.
- **One accessory, not five.** The detail is what connects, not what shouts.
- **Prioritize comfort.** The core that forces you to be uncomfortable all day isn't a style, it's a sentence.

## Common mistakes

1. **Choosing the trendy core instead of yours.** What's everywhere isn't what looks good on you.
2. **Buying the whole uniform.** You end up with a costume you wear once.
3. **Ignoring the weather.** An aesthetic doesn't keep you warm.
4. **Mixing too many references.** A look with five cores is chaos, not style.
5. **Not adapting the sizing.** Oversized clothes aren't the only way to do streetwear.

## FAQ

**Do I have to pick just one core?**
No. Most people mix two or three. What matters is that the result feels like you and not like a costume.

**Are cores only about fashion?**
No. They also include music, decor, shows and ways of speaking. Clothes are just the most visible part.

**How long does a core last?**
Some last years and others last three months. The most solid ones turn into normal clothing styles; the most viral ones burn out fast.

**Is following an aesthetic expensive?**
It can be if you buy everything at once. If you start with one piece, reuse what you own and buy secondhand, it isn't.

Which core do you like to look at but don't dare to wear? That's the one that deserves a test.
