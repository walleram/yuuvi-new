**Not all horror needs 40 hours.** Some games give you a perfect scare in an hour, leave you sleeping badly and never ask for a second season.

> Short answer: look for experiences that run one to three hours, with a clear ending, and better if they're co-op so you can play with friends.

## Why short horror works

Horror lives on tension, and tension runs out. A two-hour game can keep you alert the whole time; a twenty-hour one turns into a routine with scheduled jumpscares.

The short format is also more forgiving: if a game doesn't click, you finish it and move on. And if it scares you, you know the end is close.

## Ten games that last one night

The durations are approximate and depend on how fast you play. Availability changes by platform, so check where each one is before buying.

**1. *Iron Lung* (about 1 hour).** A tiny submarine, an ocean of blood and a camera. Almost all the fear is in what you can't see. Short, cheap and unforgettable.

**2. *The Exit 8* (about 30 minutes).** You're in a subway station and have to spot what changed in each corridor. It's more of an observation game than a jumpscare one, and it works perfectly in one sitting.

**3. *Buckshot Roulette* (about 30 minutes).** Russian roulette with a shotgun and a stranger. Pure tension, quick rounds and a grimy look that pulls you in.

**4. *Fears to Fathom* (about 1 hour per episode).** Everyday-horror anthologies: a house, a neighbor, a normal night that goes wrong. Each episode stands alone.

**5. Chilla's Art (about 1-2 hours per game).** Japanese neighborhood horror: convenience stores, trains, apartments. They aren't polished, but the atmosphere stays with you.

**6. *Poppy Playtime* (about 1 hour per chapter).** Horror and puzzles in an abandoned toy factory. The chapters are short and can be played separately.

**7. *Content Warning* (about 1-2 hours per session, co-op).** You drop into a weird world to film videos and have to come back with the camera intact. Best with friends and voice chat.

**8. *Lethal Company* (short sessions, co-op).** You collect scrap on hostile planets while something hunts you. It isn't a story, it's a loop of tension and laughter.

**9. *The Mortuary Assistant* (about 1-2 hours per shift).** You embalm bodies and, along the way, discover that one of them isn't entirely dead. Replayable and very atmospheric.

**10. *Pacify* (about 1 hour, co-op).** A haunted house, a girl and up to four players. Simple, cheap and perfect for a night of screaming.

## How to set up the night

- **Pick an order.** Start with the gentlest and save the most intense for last.
- **Dim lights, but not pitch black.** You can see the screen better and the scare still lands.
- **Headphones.** Sound is half of horror. Without them, you lose half the game.
- **Take breaks between games.** Ten minutes of light and conversation keeps the night from turning into an endurance test.
- **Give people a heads-up.** If someone doesn't handle sudden scares well, better to know before you start.

## If you're playing alone

- **Use headphones, but keep a light on.** It isn't cowardly: it's so you don't end up checking over your shoulder.
- **Set an end time.** Knowing when you'll stop makes the fear manageable.
- **Don't play right before bed.** Your brain takes a while to come down.
- **If it gets overwhelming, stop.** Horror should be fun, not a bad experience.

## Common mistakes

1. **Choosing a 20-hour game for one night.** You won't finish it and you'll feel like you didn't play anything.
2. **Playing without sound.** You miss half the design.
3. **Starting with the scariest one.** A scare too early can break the mood for the rest of the night.
4. **Not checking the platform.** Many of these are PC games and some never came to console.
5. **Forcing someone who doesn't want to.** Shared horror works when everyone is in.

## FAQ

**How long is a short horror game?**
Between 30 minutes and 3 hours. Some run 20 minutes, like *The Exit 8*, and some fill a whole night if you play several in a row.

**Are they good for people who don't play games?**
Some are, especially the co-op ones and the simple puzzle games. Others require reflexes or patience.

**Are they full of jumpscares?**
It depends. *Iron Lung* and *The Exit 8* lean on tension; *Pacify* and *Content Warning* lean on screaming and laughing.

**Can you play them on console?**
Some yes, others are PC-only. Check before you buy.

**Which one is best to start with?**
*The Exit 8* if you want something gentle, *Iron Lung* if you want atmosphere and *Content Warning* if you're playing with friends.

**Should you play them in order or pick at random?**
If you're playing several in one night, start with the gentlest and save the big scare for last. If you pick at random, warn the group about each game's tone before you start.
