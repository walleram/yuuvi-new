**A capsule wardrobe isn't owning twenty identical pieces: it's owning a few that work together.** The number matters less than the logic.

> Short answer: start with 25 or 30 pieces per season, a palette of three neutrals and two accents, and one rule: if it doesn't go with three things you already own, it doesn't come in.

## What a capsule wardrobe is (and isn't)

It's a small wardrobe designed so almost everything goes with almost everything. It isn't a minimalism contest, it isn't wearing the same outfit every day and it isn't an excuse to spend on expensive basics.

It also isn't a universal list. What works in a humid climate doesn't work in a dry one, and what works for class doesn't work for a night shift. The capsule adapts to your life, not the other way around.

## How many pieces you need

For one season, **25 or 30 pieces** is a good starting point. If you count the whole year, 40 or 50. But those numbers aren't a rule: they're a reminder that less can be enough.

The breakdown is what matters:

- **7-8 tops** you can mix with each other.
- **5-6 bottoms** (pants, skirts, shorts).
- **3-4 layers** (jacket, sweater, blazer).
- **3-4 pairs of shoes** for different contexts.
- **2-3 accessories** that change the mood.

If you live somewhere with four seasons, build two capsules a year and store the one you're not using. That way you don't count what you never wear.

## The palette: three neutrals and two accents

Pick **three neutral colors** (black, white, gray, beige, navy) and **two accents** (red, green, pink, whatever you like). With that base, almost any combination works.

The trick is to test it before you buy: grab three random pieces from your closet and see if all three work together. If the answer is no, the palette isn't working.

And you don't have to give up color. An all-black wardrobe gets boring too. Accents are what keep the capsule from looking like a uniform.

## The pieces that hold everything together

There's no magic list, but there is a structure:

- **T-shirts and tops** that survive washes and don't lose their shape.
- **One shirt** that works for dressing up and for casual days.
- **One straight or wide-leg pant** that goes with everything.
- **One jacket** that keeps you warm and doesn't clash.
- **Comfortable shoes** that are actually comfortable, not just pretty.
- **One bag or backpack** that fits your day.

From there, add what you use. If you never wear skirts, don't include a skirt "just in case." The capsule is built on your reality, not on Pinterest's.

## How to choose quality without going broke

You don't need expensive brands. You need to look at three things:

1. **The composition.** Thick cotton lasts longer than thin cotton. A fabric with elastane handles wear better.
2. **The seams.** If they're tight or poorly finished, the piece will warp soon.
3. **The care.** A piece that needs special cleaning gets worn less. If you won't care for it, don't buy it.

The useful calculation is **cost per wear**: $60 jeans you wear 100 times cost $0.60 per wear. A $15 T-shirt you wear twice costs $7.50. The second one is more expensive.

## How not to get bored in week two

A capsule gets boring if you always build the same outfit. The solution isn't buying more, it's combining better:

- **Rotate your layers.** The same T-shirt changes with a jacket, a sweater or a vest.
- **Use accessories.** A belt, a scarf or earrings change the look without changing the clothes.
- **Keep one wildcard.** Something odd, brightly colored or differently shaped that breaks the routine.
- **Swap by season.** Storing what you're not wearing makes you rediscover pieces like they're new.
- **Try new combinations at home.** Sometimes the combination you'd never think of is the one that works best.

## How to start without throwing everything out

**1. Take inventory.** Pull everything out and separate what you wear from what you don't. Don't decide yet: just look.

**2. Try the 30-day challenge.** Mark what you wear for a month. Whatever doesn't come out is a candidate to leave.

**3. Keep a box.** Put the doubtful stuff in a box and leave it closed for a month. If you don't open it, you didn't need it.

**4. Find the gaps.** Before buying, look at what you're actually missing: a jacket that works, comfortable shoes, a shirt that does everything.

**5. Buy slowly.** One good piece a month beats five bad ones in an afternoon. And secondhand is better.

## Common mistakes

1. **Buying the whole capsule at once.** You end up with pieces that aren't your style.
2. **Copying someone else's palette.** If you don't like beige, don't include it.
3. **Confusing basic with boring.** A well-chosen basic isn't a uniform.
4. **Not testing the combinations.** The capsule works in your head and fails in the mirror.
5. **Throwing out everything that doesn't fit.** Sometimes the odd piece is the one that gives you personality.

## FAQ

**How many pieces does a capsule wardrobe have?**
There's no official number. For one season, 25 or 30 is a good starting point. For the whole year, 40 or 50. What matters is that they work together and that you wear them.

**Do I have to throw out what doesn't fit?**
No. You can store it, sell it, donate it or recycle it. The capsule is a way to organize, not an obligation to get rid of everything.

**Does it work for people in a variable climate?**
Yes, but with layers. Instead of one piece per climate, choose layers you can add and remove.

**Is it more expensive?**
It can be at first, because you buy less but better. In the long run it's usually cheaper: you use everything and buy less on impulse.

Which piece do you wear the most and which the least? That difference already tells you where to start.
