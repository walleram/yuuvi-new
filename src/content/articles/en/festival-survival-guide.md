**A festival isn't about lasting the most hours, it's about arriving prepared.** The difference between an incredible memory and three days of a hangover and sore feet is what you do before.

> Short answer: plan transport, money, water, weather protection and earplugs. And don't bring anything you can't afford to lose.

## Before: tickets, transport and accommodation

**Buy your ticket from the official seller.** If it's secondhand, check that it can be transferred and that the name matches. Scams exist and are easy to avoid if you aren't in a rush.

**Think about transport before the lineup.** If the festival ends at 3 a.m. and there's no public transport, you need a plan B. Parking nearby can cost more than a shuttle bus.

**Book accommodation early.** If you're camping, read the rules: what you can bring, what time you get in, whether there are lockers. If you're sleeping off-site, book nearby and check how to get back at night.

## What to pack

You don't need half your wardrobe. What you'll actually use:

- **ID and ticket.** On your phone and on paper, in case the battery dies.
- **Water.** A reusable bottle. Many festivals have refill stations; check.
- **Food.** Fruit, nuts, something salty. Festival food is usually expensive and limited.
- **Sunscreen and a cap.** Even if it's cloudy.
- **A raincoat.** Better than an umbrella: it takes up less space and doesn't bother anyone.
- **A warm layer.** Nights change a lot.
- **Earplugs.** Good ones, not pharmacy foam.
- **A power bank and cable.** Your phone drains itself searching for signal.
- **Cash and a card.** Not every stall takes cards and the network sometimes fails.
- **A small first-aid kit.** Band-aids, painkillers, sunscreen and whatever you actually need.
- **A trash bag.** To sit on, to store things and to leave the site clean.

What you shouldn't bring: valuables, jewelry, anything you'd be sad to lose. At a festival, everything falls, gets lost and gets mixed up.

## Protect your hearing: it's the one thing that doesn't come back

A concert can go past 100 decibels, and your ears don't warn you when they're being damaged. Earplugs aren't for cowards: they're what lets you keep listening to music twenty years from now.

- **Bring uniform-reduction earplugs.** Foam ones muffle too much and sound bad.
- **Stay away from the speakers.** The middle of the crowd usually sounds better than the front row.
- **Take breaks.** Step out of a show for ten minutes and let your ears rest.
- **Don't turn the volume up to compensate.** If you hear ringing afterward, you were too close.

## Eating, drinking and keeping your energy up

- **Eat before you go in.** Arriving hungry is the fastest way to spend double.
- **Drink water between drinks.** Alcohol dehydrates and the heat does the rest.
- **Don't mix too much.** Your body doesn't need three different things in an hour.
- **Bring some sugar.** A blood-sugar crash in the middle of a crowd isn't fun.
- **Sleep when you can.** A three-day festival without sleep catches up with you.

## Money and safety

- **Don't carry all your money.** Split it between pockets, your bag and a locker.
- **Set a meeting point.** If you get separated and there's no signal, you know where to go back to.
- **Share your location with someone you trust.** And text them when you arrive.
- **Don't leave your drink unattended.** And don't accept one from someone you don't know.
- **Have a way home.** Your phone can die right when you need it.

## During: choose, rest, enjoy

- **Don't try to see everything.** It's impossible. Pick three or four shows you really care about and leave gaps.
- **Arrive early for the ones you want.** The front sections fill up.
- **Sit down between sets.** Your feet take the worst of it.
- **Don't stay in the front row all day.** The sound is worse and the crush is stronger.
- **Drink water even if you aren't thirsty.** Thirst shows up late.

## After: the way back

- **Don't drive if you've been drinking.** Not even "a little." Fatigue counts too.
- **Catch up on sleep.** Your body needs more than you think.
- **Check your hearing.** If you still hear ringing the next day, rest and avoid headphones.
- **Save your ticket and photos.** In a week you won't remember the details.

## Common mistakes

1. **Buying the ticket and ignoring transport.** The lineup doesn't get you home.
2. **Wearing new sneakers.** Your feet get destroyed. Use shoes you've already broken in.
3. **Forgetting earplugs.** Hearing doesn't come back.
4. **Spending everything on day one.** Festivals are marathons, not sprints.
5. **Not setting a meeting point.** Without signal, the group splits for good.
6. **Drinking without eating.** The dizziness hits when you least expect it.

## FAQ

**Can I bring water into the festival?**
It depends on the rules. Many allow an empty bottle and have refill stations. Check beforehand.

**What shoes are best?**
Comfortable, closed shoes you've already worn in. Sandals and new boots are a bad idea.

**Do earplugs ruin the music?**
Not if they're uniform-reduction. They lower the volume without eating the detail.

**How much money do I need?**
It depends on the festival, but budget for food, water, transport and a few extras. Keep a buffer for surprises.

**Is camping worth it?**
If you like the atmosphere and don't mind sleeping little. If you need real rest, find accommodation nearby.
