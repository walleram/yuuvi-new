**The best free apps for organizing notes are Notion, OneNote and Google Drive.** All three have a free plan and cover what you do in class: capture, sort by subject and study without paying.

> Short answer: Notion to organize everything, OneNote to write by hand on a tablet, and Google Drive to store PDFs and put together group projects.

| App | Free | Best for | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Notion** | ✅ | Organizing everything by subject | The most powerful |

The yellow ones are the trap: they download for free, but then ask for a subscription for what you actually wanted to use.

## Which app to use in each situation

**1. Live class.** Record, don't transcribe.
- Record with your phone's voice app in the background: it doesn't distract you and you don't lose a detail.
- Write down only the important stuff by hand: what the teacher underlines, dates, formulas and anything they say "will be on the test."
- At home, listen to the recording at 1.5x while you organize: ten minutes of class comes back in five.

**2. Organizing everything (exams, calendar, readings).** Notion.
- One database per subject, with three columns: content, date and status.
- Duplicate a study planner template and change the term each month.
- Exams go into a calendar view: you see the whole week and know what's coming.

**3. Handwritten notes on a tablet.** OneNote or Samsung Notes.
- Both are free and sync with your laptop and phone.
- OneNote works better with a stylus on a big screen; Samsung Notes, if you already have a Samsung.
- On several models the ink converts to text with the stylus. Good for searching, not for handing in.

**4. Group projects.** Google Docs inside Drive.
- One folder per project, with edit permissions for everyone. Never "view": then you can't touch anything.
- Split the sections: everyone writes theirs and nobody reformats someone else's.
- Comments inside the document instead of forty scattered messages.
- Put "v2" in the name instead of "final_final_good." Seriously.

**5. Library and PDFs.**
- Zotero for sources: save the PDF, rename the file with author and year, and generate the citation in Word in two clicks.
- Whatever the teacher hands out goes to Google Drive. From there you open the PDF and search inside the text.
- Two folders and no more: "Notes" and "Projects."

**6. Math, lists and random stuff.**
- Google Keep for checklists: it opens in two taps and you cross things off.
- Take a photo of a whiteboard exercise, upload it to Drive and open it with Google Lens to get the problem as text.
- With lists, your phone's keyboard gives you superscripts and weird symbols much faster than by hand.

## Tricks nobody tells you

1. **Name files with the date first**: `2026-09-29-biology-unit3`. Sorted alphabetically, they end up in chronological order.
2. **Keep a quick-capture note**: one note called just "inbox." Dump whatever comes up in class there and sort it into subjects on Fridays. Ten minutes a week.
3. **Color rules**: one color per subject, and use it in the calendar and the note title too. You find the topic in two seconds.
4. **Reminders go with margin, not on the exact day**: one at 7 days and one at 48 hours. The first is to start, the second is to review.
5. **Search inside your own PDFs**: Drive's search finds the word inside the document. If the teacher said "this will be on the test" and you can't remember, search for "test."
6. **Anki only for what you struggle with**: fifteen cards a day, not one more. On Android it's free with AnkiDroid; on iOS it's paid.
7. **The one thing you can't recreate**: a one-line summary per class. Everything else can be redone. That summary is what you review at four in the morning.
8. **Two-minute backup**: export your notes to PDF once a month and email them to yourself. If you lose your phone, you don't lose the term.

## The minimum setup (100% free)

- **Phone**: built-in voice recorder for class and Google Keep for lists.
- **Laptop**: Notion or OneNote as a single archive. Just one, not five.
- **Cloud**: one Google Drive folder per subject.
- **Calendar**: the one on your phone, with every exam set to a double alarm.
- **Zotero**: only if you're working on a thesis. Before that, it's overkill.

With this you have more than enough for your first midterm. 💪

## FAQ

**What's the best free app for notes?**
It depends on how you study. For handwriting, OneNote or Samsung Notes. To see everything organized with a calendar, Notion. To store the PDF the teacher sent, Google Drive. Short answer: pick one of the three, not all three at once.

**Is Notion actually free?**
Yes, for personal use. It gives you pages, databases and file uploads without you hitting the limit in everyday use. Paid features show up in collaboration and some templates. If you're setting it up for a group project, check first what plan the shared account requires.

**With a free tablet I can already write like on paper, right?**
Yes. OneNote and Samsung Notes let you write with the stylus and sync with your laptop without paying anything. The barrier appears if you want handwriting recognition and premade templates: that's where GoodNotes is paid, and its free download includes very few notebooks.

**What do I do if I lose my phone with all my notes?**
That's why the first two rules exist: date-first names and everything in the cloud. Notion, OneNote and Google Drive keep your material in your account, so you log in from your laptop and get it back. As extra insurance, email it to yourself once a month.

**Obsidian or Notion?**
Notion if you want something visual, with calendar views and ready-made databases. Obsidian if you prefer your notes to be text files on your disk, working offline and not depending on any company. For high school, Notion is easier.
