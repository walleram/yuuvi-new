**Your first RPG shouldn't last 100 hours or have twelve menus before the first fight.** Start with something short, with adjustable difficulty and a story that pushes you forward.

> Short answer: pick an RPG that runs 10 to 30 hours, lower the difficulty if you need to, and don't try to do every side quest.

## What an RPG is (without the encyclopedia)

RPG stands for role-playing game: a game where you play a character, make decisions and improve their skills. The key isn't fantasy or swords: it's that **you build the character** and the game reacts.

There are several types, and it helps to know them before choosing:

- **JRPG.** Japanese origins, a very guided story, turn-based combat. Examples: *Persona*, *Final Fantasy*, *Pokémon*.
- **CRPG.** Closer to tabletop role-playing, with lots of decisions and text. Example: *Baldur's Gate 3*.
- **Action RPG.** Real-time combat with progression. Examples: *Skyrim*, *The Witcher*, *Hades*.
- **Tactical RPG.** Turn-based combat on a grid. Example: *Fire Emblem*.
- **Roguelike.** You die, start over and improve between runs. Example: *Hades*.

## Three questions before you choose

**1. How much time do you have?** If you play an hour every two days, an 80-hour RPG becomes a chore. Look for something in the 10-to-30-hour range.

**2. What platform do you play on?** PC, Switch, PlayStation and Xbox have different libraries. Before you fall in love with a title, check that it's on yours.

**3. How many menus can you tolerate?** Some games teach the system step by step; others drop twenty tabs on you in the tutorial. If it's your first RPG, start with the former.

## Five RPGs to start with

**1. *Undertale* (about 8-10 hours).** Turn-based combat, moral decisions and a tone that looks simple but isn't. It's short, cheap and perfect for understanding what an RPG is without committing to a saga.

**2. *Child of Light* (about 12 hours).** Turn-based combat, storybook art and a very direct story. Ideal if you want something pretty, short and without an endless inventory.

**3. *Pokémon* (about 20-30 hours).** The easiest RPG to start with: it teaches the rules without overwhelming you and you can play at your own pace. Any recent entry works.

**4. *Sea of Stars* (about 30 hours).** A tribute to classic JRPGs, with turn-based combat and exploration. If you like the style, it's a very polished doorway in.

**5. *Hades* (about 20-30 hours).** An action roguelike RPG: you die, come back and uncover more story. It's more demanding, but it teaches progression in a very addictive way.

And one for later: *Baldur's Gate 3*. It's excellent, but it's long and complex. Save it for when you already know what an RPG is and want a big one.

## How not to quit in the first few hours

- **Follow the main quest.** Side quests are fine, but early on they scatter your attention. Stick to the story until you understand the system.
- **Lower the difficulty.** It isn't cheating: it's removing a barrier so you can learn the rules.
- **Play in short sessions.** An hour a day gets you further than a six-hour marathon that leaves you fried.
- **Take notes.** Quests, names and items. RPGs throw a lot of information at you and memory fails.
- **Don't restart over one decision.** Almost no RPG breaks because you picked one option. Move on and learn.

## Common mistakes

1. **Starting with the biggest RPG.** *Persona 5*, *Baldur's Gate 3* or *Skyrim* are enormous. They aren't bad first RPGs, but they're hard to finish.
2. **Doing every side quest.** The map fills with icons and the story goes cold.
3. **Skipping the tutorials.** They feel slow, but they explain the system you'll use for 30 hours.
4. **Comparing your run to someone else's.** RPGs are played differently. Someone else's build doesn't mean yours is wrong.
5. **Quitting in the first hour.** RPGs usually start slow. Give them two sessions before deciding.

## FAQ

**Does an RPG have to be fantasy?**
No. There are sci-fi RPGs, horror RPGs, school RPGs and even farming RPGs. Fantasy is the most common setting, not a requirement.

**Can I start with a sequel?**
Sometimes, but it isn't ideal. If the story matters, look up the order. If you only want the system, many sequels stand alone.

**Are RPGs hard?**
It depends on the game. Many have adjustable difficulty or accessible modes. The toughest are tactical games and roguelikes.

**How long is an RPG?**
From 8 hours to more than 100. To start, look for something in the 10-to-30-hour range.

**Will it hook me if I've never played one?**
If you pick a short one with a clear story, yes. Your first RPG doesn't have to be your favorite game: it has to show you whether you like the genre.
